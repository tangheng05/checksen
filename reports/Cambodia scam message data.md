# Cambodia's real scam data exposes CheckSen's blind spots

No public Khmer scam-message dataset exists, and verbatim Khmer scam text is a **known gap in this research**: the Khmer-text researcher stalled twice before finishing, and only **five verbatim Khmer lures** turned up, mostly 2023–2024 Telegram-takeover and fake-aid messages quoted by CamCERT and Fact Crescendo. The usable real material sits elsewhere. It includes bank-published example scams (Wing), lures quoted by ministries and regulators (MPTC, TRC, the Anti-Cyber Crime Department), lookalike domains from bank warnings and urlscan.io, and a surprisingly rich set of **real legitimate bank and telco messages that read like scams**. I merged 104 labelled examples from the notes (63 scam, 41 legit, 60 verbatim) and ran them through CheckSen's current rules. On the 55 Cambodian scam rows the text rules caught **24%**, or **30% of the 44 full messages**, against **96.7%** on the self-written v0 set. They also flagged **9 of 41 legitimate messages (22%)**, seven of them real bank lucky-draw promotions. The link rules flagged **15 of 54 official hosts**, including ACLEDA's own internet-banking login site and nine banks' Bakong gateways, and missed **21 of 62** reported or suspicious phishing domains. The largest gains come from cheap fixes: multi-domain allow-lists, a prize rule that looks for claim fees, freeze and re-verify wording, and Telegram-takeover rules. v0 needs a real-message, real-false-positive v1 beside it.

## No public Khmer corpus exists, so the benchmark must be hand-built

Searches of HuggingFace, GitHub and academic indexes found **no Khmer-language SMS or Telegram scam dataset**. The closest items are an empty repo named `spam_dataset_khmer_english` ([GitHub](https://github.com/reanyouda/spam_dataset_khmer_english)), an MIT CoreML Khmer SMS classifier that ships no training data ([GitHub](https://github.com/VatanaChhorn/KhmerCoreMLSMSClassifier)), and a student project whose 16 Khmer rows are hand-written, not real ([GitHub](https://github.com/Tanailee/khmer-english-scam-safety-assistant)). CheckSen's own `evals/data/v0.jsonl` is already ahead of anything public. But its 100 rows are 75 synthetic and 25 reconstructed, with **zero verbatim messages**, and the rules' author wrote them.

The redistributable outside data is thin and mostly off-target. The best Southeast Asian set is a **2026 Vietnamese SMS phishing dataset**: 2,991 real messages, 798 scam, **CC BY 4.0**, with PII tokenised ([HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/trannguyenthaituan/vietnamese_sms_dataset)). Its scam types mirror Cambodia's: lookalike bank domains such as `vietcombank.vn-gll.top`, fake police e-ID notices on `chinhphu-gov.vip`, like-for-pay TikTok task jobs, and 30%-a-month investment offers. English options are old and UK-centric. Mishra & Soni's smishing set (5,971 messages, **CC BY 4.0**, [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/f45bkkt8pr/1)) and the UCI SMS Spam Collection (**CC BY 4.0**, 2000s premium-rate spam, [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/228/sms+spam+collection)) can be vendored with attribution. Smishtank is more modern but **CC BY-NC-SA**, so fetch it at eval time and keep it out of git ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18430)). A 50-file Indonesian loan and prize set is **CC0** ([GitHub](https://github.com/bopbi/indonesia-sms-spam-dataset)). I found no Thai, Lao or Malay SMS scam text corpus. The Philippine Kaggle sets have unverified licenses.

The single most Cambodia-relevant dataset is Moses Ngeth's: **4,991 scam-compound job ads from 37 Telegram channels**, whose full anonymised text the report calls "public" with "requests … addressed to the author" ([report PDF](https://cms.mosesngeth.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cutting_the_Grass_Not_the_Root.pdf)). These ads recruit workers into compounds rather than targeting CheckSen's users. They would still anchor a recruitment or trafficking category. For Khmer text handling, SIL's MIT-licensed normalizer resolves visually identical but differently encoded clusters (for example "ខែ្មរ" → "ខ្មែរ") ([GitHub](https://github.com/sillsdev/khmer-normalizer)). Scammers could exploit exactly that gap against substring rules.

## Authorities quote Telegram takeovers verbatim; everything else survives as paraphrase

Khmer coverage is the weakest part of this research, and the reason is structural. Cambodian banks, telcos and police post warnings as **Facebook images**, which search engines do not index. Khmer Times and Fresh News block fetchers, and Kampuchea Thmey renders article bodies in JavaScript. So the verbatim Khmer found comes to two Telegram-takeover lures quoted by CamCERT, one fake French-aid message, a fake Coca-Cola survey title and two scratch-lottery page names. The first CamCERT lure reads "…គណនីរបស់អ្នកមិនត្រូវបានផ្ទៀងផ្ទាត់អស់រយៈពេលជាយូរ… ប្រព័ន្ធនឹងលុបចោលគណនីដោយបង្ខំបន្ទាប់ពី 24 ម៉ោង" ([CamCERT, 2023-08-22](https://www.camcert.gov.kh/telegram-phishing-2023/)). The second threatens deletion "ក្នុងរយៈពេល 24 ម៉ោង" and links to `telegrams.org-web.net` ([CamCERT, 2023-07-25](https://www.camcert.gov.kh/telegram-phishing-23/)). The French-aid message promised "$1.500" per Cambodian ([Fact Crescendo](https://cambodia.factcrescendo.com/a-scam-of-french-donation-spreading-on-telegram/)). **No verbatim Khmer text was found for fake loans, fake police, OTP theft, money mules, family impersonation or fake jobs.** These are the categories CheckSen targets most, so Khmer recall on them stays unmeasured until real messages are collected.

English material is richer but mostly illustrative. **Wing Bank is the only bank that publishes sample scam texts**: "You've won a cash prize from Wing Bank. Pay 50,000 Riel to claim!", "Instant loan approved! Just send your ID and pay the small service fee.", "We can help you withdraw your Wing account money faster — share your OTP." and "Click this link to verify your Wing account security." ([Wing Bank](https://www.wingbank.com.kh/en/company/security-awareness-center/scam-awareness)). ACLEDA names the exact phrases its customers see in scam SMS: "to verify again to protect your rights" and "please re-verify the account with 24 hours". It also says its only SMS sender is "ACLEDA" ([ACLEDA](https://www.acledabank.com.kh/kh/eng/security_tip_phishing)). ABA publishes lookalike domains rather than message bodies ([ABA](https://www.ababank.com/en/aba-news/be-aware-of-financial-scammers/)). The MPTC's English quote of the Telegram lure ends "click on the following link within 24 hours, or your account will be deleted" ([Phnom Penh Post](https://www.phnompenhpost.com/national/ministry-issue-scam-warning-telegram-users)). The Anti-Cyber Crime Department's 2025 prize lure reads "I won a reward just for answering some questions! You should do it now before the prize presentation ceremony ends!" ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/crime/cambodia-arrests-2-for-using-hun-sen-s-name-in-180-000-telegram-scam-4853163.html)). **Smart, Cellcard and Metfone have no indexed scam warnings at all.** Every telco-side warning found came from the TRC or the MPTC. Fake-loan, fake-bank-staff and debt-shaming scripts exist only as Khmer Times search summaries, so the JSONL below carries them as reconstructions (`verbatim: false`).

## Scam types in 2025–2026 have outgrown CheckSen's eight categories

CheckSen's categories (loan, job, otp, prize, account, authority, investment, mule) cover the classic Cambodian patterns. The 2025–2026 record adds several vectors that no rule touches. The **Telegram account takeover** is the most important, because it opens the way to other scams: family impersonation, loan spam and investment spam sent from hijacked contacts. The TRC's September 2025 notice names three Telegram tactics: impersonating senior officials for "high returns in a short period", prize messages requesting personal data, and fake Telegram security threats "demanding passwords" ([TRC](https://www.trc.gov.kh/en/media/news-releases/7-news-release/)). In November 2025 police seized a car-mounted **fake base station (SMS blaster)** in Phnom Penh. It had been sending gambling SMS and fake Telegram-security messages since March ([CEN](https://cen.com.kh/archives/875821.html)). So scam SMS no longer needs a telco sender at all.

The categories in the brief have mixed evidence. **Fake loans with upfront fees** have strong evidence: police warned of loans "up to $2,500" with a "$500" credit-check fee (Khmer Times, search summary only, [link](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/501936986/police-issue-warning-over-fake-loans-and-bank-officers/)), and ababank.biz carried the title "ABA Bank - កម្ចីរហ័ស និងងាយស្រួល" in August 2026. **Fake parcels** have the weakest: Cambodia Post warned of a fake parcel-payment site in April 2025, but its domain and message text were unretrievable ([Khmer Times](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/501662773/cambodia-post-warns-of-fake-website/)). **Family impersonation** rests on an NBC warning that scammers send "fake voice messages" posing as relatives ([EAC News](https://eacnews.asia/home/details/21045)), with no Cambodian message text. For fake KHQR payment screenshots, sextortion and recovery scams I found **no Cambodia-specific 2025–2026 evidence**. The only KHQR-specific warning is ABA's notice about QR stickers pasted over merchant codes ([ABA](https://www.ababank.com/en/aba-news/protect-yourself-from-qr-code-scams/)). CheckSen's receiver-name display already addresses that case.

## Real bank promotions and alerts read exactly like scams

The best verbatim Cambodian text found is **legitimate**, and it attacks CheckSen's prize and fee rules directly. Vattanac Bank announced "Congratulations! We are delighted to announce our lucky draw winners". Its KHQR campaign sent merchants to "https://bit.ly/Register-KHQR", and its terms promise merchants "a chance to double their money weekly" ([Vattanac](https://www.vattanacbank.com/merchants/week-7-lucky-draw-winners-for-vattanac-bank-khqr-merchant-campaign)). KB PRASAC opens with "Win! Win! Win!" and "ឈ្នះ! ឈ្នះ! ឈ្នះ! ឱកាសឈ្នះរង្វាន់…" ([KB PRASAC](https://www.kbprasacbank.com.kh/km/media/promotion/lucky-draw-for-kb-prasac-merchants/)). BIDC ran "You win a Toyota Raize – I win 150$" with a "lucky code" ([BIDC](https://www.bidc.com.kh/en/article/minigame-you-win-a-toyota-raize-i-win-150)). Smart invites users to "Log in to SmartNas every day for your chance to win" ([Smart](https://www.smart.com.kh/promotions)). What separates these from scams is consistent: **no real campaign charges a claim fee**, and Vattanac states that funds are "automatically credited to the winners' accounts". Seven of these promos fired PrizeBait alone, which scores 0.4 and so a Suspicious verdict.

Transaction and service messages cause the second group of false positives. ABA merchant alerts follow a stable shape, "`$2.70 paid by CUSTOMER (*098) on Sep 05, 04:09 PM via ABA PAY at MERCHANT. Remark: . Trx. ID: 178859934628331, APV: 964372.`" ([GitHub parser tests](https://github.com/Terminators-S/khqr-Tele-webhook)). Their 6-digit APV codes sit next to money words, which OTP-style rules must not treat as codes. BIDC's July 2026 notice says Bakong and KHQR transfers "will be temporarily suspended" for maintenance, in English and in Khmer ("ផ្អាកដំណើរការជាបណ្តោះអាសន្ន"). The Khmer version contains **real spelling slips** ("តំហែទាំ", "អាស្រយ័"), so typos cannot count as a scam signal ([BIDC KM](https://www.bidc.com.kh/km/article/bakong-khqr-system-maintenance-3-hours-from-22h00-on-30-07-2026-to-01h00-on-31-07-2026)). CheckSen correctly left the BIDC notice alone, because AccountThreat also needs a verify or link term. It did flag ACLEDA's description of its SMS alerts as UpfrontFee, because the text contains "cash deposit" and "overdraft loan" ([ACLEDA](https://www.acledabank.com.kh/kh/eng/ps_ebsmsalert)). Two further legitimate collisions need handling. First, **Telegram charges Cambodian numbers about $1 for Premium before it will send the login SMS**, a real fee attached to a login code ([Cambodia Expats Online](https://cambodiaexpatsonline.com/ask-the-expats-questions-answers/telegram-sms-extortion-fee-any-workarounds-t61865.html)). Second, traffic fines are now legitimately payable through Canadia Bank within 15 days ([Khmer Times](https://www.khmertimeskh.com/502018036/cambodia-tightens-traffic-law-enforcement-expands-fine-payment-options)). Verbatim OTP and alert SMS from ABA, ACLEDA, Wing, Bakong and the three telcos were **not found anywhere public**. The only way to get them is to screenshot the team's own phones.

## Link rules flag ACLEDA's own login site while missing "my" and "the" prefixes

`BRANDS` in `src/analyzers/link.rs` allows one official domain per brand. Anything carrying the brand stem is therefore treated as a lookalike, including the official ACLEDA domains verified live on 2026-09-28: **acledainternetbank.com.kh** (the login site), acledabankmb.com.kh, acledasecurities.com.kh and ACLEDA Laos's .com.la domains. The `bakong` token check flags other banks' Bakong gateways too, such as bakong.maybank2u.com.kh, bakong.icbc.com.kh, bakong.cimbcambodia.com and six more. vnet.wingbank.com, a Cambodia-hosted Wing host seen on urlscan, is flagged as well. In the other direction, **21 of 62** reported or suspicious phishing hosts passed clean. Some misses carry the brand behind a prefix (theababank.com, myababank.com). Some split or transpose it (acle-da.com, kh.bank.aceldaa.com, wimngeacash.com). The rest imitate brands CheckSen doesn't know: canadias.com, vattanaccommercialbank.com, chipmongcommercialbank.com, hatthabanks.com, sathapanabank.com, telegrams.org-web.net, cambodia-e-arrival.com and cambodiaimmigration.org. The aceldaa miss has a concrete cause: `starts_with_misspelling` compares the label against "acledabank" (10 characters) instead of the stem "acleda", so no short transposed label can match.

The confirmed-malicious list is short: ABA's published ababkonline.com, ababnkonline.com and four `.xyz` sender domains ([ABA](https://www.ababank.com/en/aba-news/phishing-security-advisory/)), plus the Immigration Department's cambodia-e-arrival.com ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/cambodias-immigration-department-warns-of-illegal-arrival-website/)). urlscan.io adds an unconfirmed but coherent 2025–2026 cluster. It includes **seven Wing "lucky wheel" domains registered within three weeks of October 2025**, ABA quick-loan and "Statement Verification" pages, ACLEDA login clones on `.com` instead of `.com.kh`, and brand-named Zendesk trial subdomains ([urlscan](https://urlscan.io/api/v1/search/?q=page.title:%22Wing%20Bank%22)). urlscan's automated tagger also flagged **official hatthabank.com hosts**, so tier-B feed hits cannot count as ground truth. The allow-list rule that follows from the evidence is "registrable domain in an explicit list, or any `*.gov.kh`". It must **not** extend to all of `*.com.kh`, because Phishing.Database lists compromised `.com.kh` hosts ([Phishing.Database](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Phishing-Database/Phishing.Database/master/phishing-domains-ACTIVE.txt)).

On domain age, **`.kh` is absent from the IANA RDAP bootstrap** (publication of 2026-09-16), and IANA lists no WHOIS server for it ([IANA dns.json](https://data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json); [IANA .kh](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/kh.html)). CheckSen's `domain_age_days` therefore returns `None` for every `.kh` name, and `NewDomain` fires only on `Some(days < 30)`. The current behaviour is correct: unknown age is not new. It is only implicit, though, and no test pins it. Nearly all observed lookalikes sit on `.com`, `.xyz`, `.online` and similar suffixes, where RDAP works, so the age check covers exactly the domains that matter.

## Conclusion

The real data reverses the picture v0 gives. CheckSen's rules are precise on the scams their author imagined, but real Cambodian data mostly consists of messages the rules were never shown. Real scam wording clusters around **account freezing, Telegram verification and prize claims**, the areas where current regexes are thinnest. Real legitimate wording clusters around **lucky draws, "temporarily suspended" notices and payment alerts**, where the rules overreach. The fixes are therefore less about new keywords than about requiring a second, costly act: pay to claim, verify through a link, or send a code. The link analyzer should also know every domain a bank actually uses. The largest open risk remains Khmer. Until verbatim Khmer loan, police, mule and family scripts are collected from screenshots and consented forwards, CheckSen's Khmer recall is an estimate, not a measurement.
